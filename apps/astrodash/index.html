The planets now

Planet calendar

Greatest elongations, oppositions and conjunctions, next 14 months

Elongation is the angle from the Sun. Mercury and Venus are best at greatest elongation (but see the viewing windows below). The outer planets are best at opposition, when they are opposite the Sun, up all night, nearest to Earth and brightest.

Jupiter and its moons

Next three days

Green bar: Jupiter 10° up and the Sun below −6°

Positions come from a low-accuracy model (typically within 0.2 Jupiter radii), so transit and occultation times can be off by several minutes. Shadow transits, which need the Sun-Jupiter geometry, are not included. Any telescope shows the four moons; 100 mm and a steady night starts to show a transit of Ganymede or Io's dark shadow.

Where and how to look

For a camera, start at about 15 s, f/2.8, ISO 1600 to 3200 with a wide lens, then shorten to 2 to 6 s once the aurora brightens and starts to move. Face the glow, check your phone's night mode can go to 10 s or more, and give your eyes 15 minutes to adapt. Aurora often looks grey to the eye and green on a sensor.
